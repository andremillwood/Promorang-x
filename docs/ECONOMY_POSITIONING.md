# Promorang Economy Positioning

This document is the working source of truth for how Promorang should explain its participant economy in public-facing copy.

## Core loop

`Moments -> verified participation -> Marks -> Points / Keys / Pieces -> PromoShare -> Gems -> stronger network growth`

Promorang should present the economy as a closed loop around real participation, not as speculative earnings.

## Payment Standard

Promorang should use one clear rule for paid platform value:

- Gems are the platform spending currency.
- Users can buy Gems directly with payment processing.
- Tier subscriptions can be paid directly with payment processing.
- Pieces are bought, sold, and supported with Gems.
- Rewards, boosts, premium marketplace actions, liquidity participation, and funded value loops should be denominated in Gems unless there is a specific payment-processing reason not to.
- Keys are not payment currency. Keys unlock access, reduce friction, qualify status, or prove participant intent.
- Points are not payment currency. Points measure activity, progression, and reputation.

In product copy, use this distinction:

- `Gems pay.`
- `Keys unlock.`
- `Points prove activity.`
- `Pieces expose users and stakeholders to the value profile of moments, content, hosts, and venues.`

## What users earn

### Points

- Points reward participation, check-ins, content, referrals, and repeat activity.
- Points are the broadest reward and should be communicated as always-on progression.

### Keys

- Keys unlock access, status, and better opportunities.
- Keys should be framed as tier and access currency, not payout currency.

### Pieces

- Pieces represent participation-linked or performance-linked ownership signals inside the platform.
- Public messaging should explain two participant-facing piece paths clearly:
  - Early participant pieces: for people who showed up early and verified into a moment or content arc.
  - Power performer pieces: for users whose referrals, content, conversions, or repeat movement materially outperform.
- Complementary pieces can be issued to both early participants and power performers when a moment, creator, or campaign supports them.

### PromoShare

- PromoShare is the recurring reward layer that turns verified contribution into cycle-based eligibility.
- PromoShare should be described as a qualified reward system, not guaranteed income.
- Eligibility can expand based on standing, contribution quality, referrals, repeat participation, and campaign rules.
- Gem/cash PromoShare should require active Plus standing or higher.
- Active standing can be paid or earned: free users can unlock temporary higher standing by earning enough Keys through verified activity in a monthly period.
- Paid participant tiers should contribute a configured percentage of subscription revenue into PromoShare reward pools, liquidity reserves, and optional local impact pools.

### Gems

- Gems are Promorang's spendable internal value unit.
- Public messaging should use one canonical rule: `1 Gem = $1.00 USD`.
- Gems are the preferred way to denominate funded rewards, promotional credits, marketplace spend, and redemptions.
- Pieces interactions should be Gem-based: buy pieces with Gems, sell pieces for Gems, and add liquidity with Pieces plus Gems.

## Network growth and affiliate framing

Promorang should not hide referral growth as a backend-only mechanic.

The public model should be:

- Users grow a network, crew, or audience around moments and creators they support.
- That network growth can improve standing, piece eligibility, PromoShare relevance, and Gems rewards.
- Promorang benefits because trusted participants bring more participants, creators, and venues into the graph.

Recommended language:

- `Grow your network`
- `Build your crew`
- `Increase your standing`
- `Turn trusted participation into qualified upside`

Avoid thin affiliate language that makes the system sound like generic referral spam.

## Public claims to avoid

Do not publicly promise:

- fixed `earnings per Mark`
- fixed cash returns by tier
- vague `real money` claims without qualification
- floating Gem value unless it is truly implemented

Avoid messaging like:

- `$2-4 per Mark`
- `earn real money just for showing up`
- `guaranteed payout`

## Feasible reward framing

The defensible version of the economy is:

- user activity must be verified
- rewards must be tied to funded pools, campaigns, or platform budgets
- rewards should be capped and qualified
- participant upside should be denominated in Gems where possible

This keeps the model operationally and narratively tied to real value creation rather than open-ended promises.

## Marketing implications

Homepage and stakeholder pages should consistently answer:

- What is a Mark?
- What are Pieces?
- How do early participants benefit?
- How do power performers benefit?
- What is PromoShare?
- Why are Gems used?
- How does growing your network increase your upside?

If a page cannot answer those questions, it is underselling the product.
