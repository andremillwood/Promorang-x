# Promorang Category Map

_Status: Strategic taxonomy / platform expansion guide_

## 1. Purpose

This document expands the Momentum Engine beyond an event-centric interpretation of `moments`.

Promorang should not treat a moment as only:

- an event
- a meetup
- a pop-up
- a social gathering

It should treat a moment as:

`a structured real-world interaction, anchored to a place, time window, or proof system, with reward, memory, attribution, and loyalty potential`

That framing allows Promorang to work across:

- event-heavy venues
- retail spaces
- supermarkets and CPG environments
- service businesses
- wellness and appointment businesses
- creator-driven hybrid missions
- neighborhood infrastructure

This category map is meant to guide:

- product design
- stakeholder UX
- GTM prioritization
- moment taxonomy
- proof design
- reward system design
- content and creator mechanics
- agent-facing opportunity modeling

## 2. Core Modeling Principle

Promorang should classify opportunities on two axes:

1. `Venue Category`
What kind of place or operator is involved?

2. `Moment Archetype`
What kind of human behavior is being programmed?

This is more powerful than a single category field.

Examples:

- `retail + drop`
- `barber + ritual`
- `grocery + sampling`
- `fitness + gathering`
- `creator + content`
- `cafe + founder unlock`

## 3. Moment Archetypes

These archetypes should exist across the platform regardless of venue category.

### 3.1 Gathering Moment

Best for:

- density
- synchronized presence
- atmosphere
- viral physical coordination

Examples:

- language exchange at a cafe
- sneaker drop queue
- creator meetup at a store
- community run club

Best proof patterns:

- geofence
- QR scan
- host verification
- density threshold logic

Primary stakeholder upside:

- `Participant`: social energy, live bonus, status
- `Host`: visible momentum and atmosphere
- `Venue`: floor traffic and story density
- `Brand`: collective ROI and social proof
- `Agency`: launch spectacle and recap value

### 3.2 Visit Moment

Best for:

- simple foot traffic
- first visit
- repeat visit
- traffic qualification

Examples:

- visit the boutique between 2 PM and 5 PM
- enter the pharmacy and scan the wellness shelf
- arrive at the venue and confirm presence

Best proof patterns:

- geofence
- venue QR
- timestamped entry

Primary stakeholder upside:

- `Participant`: low-friction entry reward
- `Venue`: measurable visit lift
- `Brand`: proof of physical exposure
- `Agency`: easy baseline attribution

### 3.3 Service Moment

Best for:

- appointment businesses
- personal service operators
- recurring service behavior

Examples:

- Tuesday haircut loyalty check-in
- salon transformation reward
- spa recovery visit
- dental or wellness attendance loop

Best proof patterns:

- booking confirmation
- staff QR/PIN confirmation
- service completion verification

Primary stakeholder upside:

- `Participant`: repeat loyalty and premium treatment
- `Service operator`: retention and upsell
- `Brand`: sponsorship of trusted recurring behavior

### 3.4 Drop Moment

Best for:

- scarcity
- urgency
- launch windows
- exclusive access

Examples:

- limited fashion rack drop
- first 20 shoppers unlock founder status
- weekend beauty kit release
- creator merch pickup

Best proof patterns:

- scan + purchase verification
- check-in + stock threshold
- queue position or early-entry validation

Primary stakeholder upside:

- `Participant`: exclusivity and founder identity
- `Brand`: launch heat
- `Retailer`: traffic and conversion compression
- `Creator`: hype and conversion

### 3.5 Ritual Moment

Best for:

- repeated behaviors
- habit loops
- loyalty memory building

Examples:

- weekly coffee ritual
- every-third-cut barber loyalty
- monthly grocery family basket challenge
- recurring yoga attendance streak

Best proof patterns:

- repeat check-ins
- recurrence counter
- streak logic

Primary stakeholder upside:

- `Participant`: streaks, habit status, perks
- `Venue`: retention
- `Brand`: repeat behavior sponsorship

### 3.6 Content Moment

Best for:

- online-to-offline journeys
- creator-driven activation
- hybrid proof flows

Examples:

- watch creator vlog, unlock hidden in-store action
- reveal a secret phrase in content, redeem in person
- comment or share digitally, then check in physically

Best proof patterns:

- content engagement log
- mission attribution record
- physical completion proof

Primary stakeholder upside:

- `Creator`: O2O conversion proof
- `Participant`: hybrid unlocks and co-branded memory
- `Brand`: attributable story-to-store conversion
- `Agency`: better reporting and creator packaging

### 3.7 Sampling Moment

Best for:

- CPG trials
- demos
- physical product touchpoints

Examples:

- try new beverage at supermarket tasting station
- skincare sampling in retail
- product demo at pharmacy or beauty counter

Best proof patterns:

- QR scan at sampling table
- photo or staff validation
- follow-up redemption

Primary stakeholder upside:

- `Brand`: verified trial, not just impression
- `Venue`: activation energy
- `Participant`: free product + memory + perk

### 3.8 Appointment Moment

Best for:

- booking-led businesses
- time-slot utilization
- off-peak demand shaping

Examples:

- book a quiet-hour salon slot
- barber off-peak bonus booking
- wellness screening appointment unlock

Best proof patterns:

- booking ID
- staff confirmation
- check-in plus completion

Primary stakeholder upside:

- `Service operator`: smoother demand distribution
- `Participant`: better value and loyalty access

### 3.9 Referral Moment

Best for:

- social spread
- viral acquisition
- community growth

Examples:

- bring a friend to the studio
- invite a first-time shopper
- shared creator mission completion

Best proof patterns:

- referral linkage
- downstream join attribution
- dual completion logic

Primary stakeholder upside:

- `Participant`: catalyst identity and viral upside
- `Venue`: lower-cost growth
- `Brand`: friend-driven acquisition

### 3.10 Founder / Mayor Moment

Best for:

- cold start
- community identity
- local positioning

Examples:

- first 25 verified members at a new venue
- first district founder series
- become the founding mayor of a neighborhood node

Best proof patterns:

- sequence-based verification
- geography or venue-based eligibility
- identity-bound memory issuance

Primary stakeholder upside:

- `Participant`: status and permanent symbolic ownership
- `Venue`: local evangelists
- `Brand`: early community stickiness
- `Agency`: launch narrative

## 5. Stakeholder View by Category

Every category should be evaluated against all core stakeholders.

### 5.1 Participant

Participants care most about:

- reward clarity
- status
- repeatability
- local relevance
- memory value
- social proof

Best participant categories:

- food and beverage
- beauty/grooming
- fitness/wellness
- fashion retail
- entertainment

### 5.2 Host

Hosts care most about:

- turnout
- atmosphere
- coordination
- proof review simplicity
- repeatable format

Best host categories:

- food and beverage
- community/civic
- entertainment
- fitness

### 5.3 Venue / Merchant / Service Operator

They care most about:

- foot traffic
- repeat visits
- proof simplicity
- operational calm
- redemption clarity

Best categories:

- food and beverage
- barber/salon/spa
- grocery
- boutique retail
- fitness studios

### 5.4 Brand

Brands care most about:

- hard attribution
- conversion quality
- sampling
- creator fit
- repeat behavior

Best brand categories:

- grocery and CPG
- beauty retail
- fashion/sneakers
- food and beverage
- wellness

### 5.5 Creator

Creators care most about:

- storyability
- visible identity
- audience fit
- O2O conversion
- sponsorship leverage

Best creator categories:

- fashion
- beauty
- food
- fitness
- entertainment

### 5.6 Agency

Agencies care most about:

- repeatable packaging
- client reporting
- creator coordination
- venue readiness
- proof confidence

Best agency categories:

- fashion retail
- beauty
- food and beverage
- grocery/CPG
- hospitality launches

### 5.7 Agent Layer

AI agents care most about:

- structured opportunity types
- proof requirements
- optimization parameters
- urgency
- expected value

Best categories for early agent surfaces:

- visit moments
- appointment moments
- drop moments
- ritual moments
- creator content moments

These are easier to structure than ambiguous community gatherings.

- `discount`

- `sample`
- `access`
- `perk`
- `memory`
- `status`
- `exclusive content`
- `appointment privilege`
- `founder identity`
- `bundle unlock`

## 7. Proof Type Matrix

Proof should vary by category and archetype.

### Low-friction proof

- geofence
- venue QR
- rotating staff code

Best for:

- visit
- ritual
- gathering

### Commerce-sensitive proof

- QR + purchase verification
- receipt or basket proof
- POS-linked validation

Best for:

- grocery
- retail
- CPG sampling

### Service-sensitive proof

- booking reference
- staff confirmation
- appointment completion

Best for:

- barber
- salon
- wellness

### Hybrid proof

- content engagement record
- mission attribution
- physical completion proof

Best for:

- content moments
- creator journeys

## 8. GTM Priority Ranking

The following ranking balances:

- repeat behavior
- creator fit
- brand spend potential
- proof feasibility
- stakeholder clarity

### Tier 1

- food and beverage
- beauty and grooming
- fitness and wellness
- boutique retail / fashion / sneakers
- grocery / CPG sampling

Why:

- high frequency
- easy story hooks
- strong creator compatibility
- loyalty and memory potential
- brand and agency demand

### Tier 2

- entertainment and culture
- community and civic hubs
- coworking / learning / networking

Why:

- strong community and founder potential
- good density mechanics
- lower operational predictability in some cases

### Tier 3

- hospitality and travel
- family and kids
- auto and mobility
- home/showroom

Why:

- viable but less obvious as an early wedge
- may need more category-specific onboarding and ops language

### Tier 4 / Selective

- light healthcare
- regulated or privacy-sensitive environments

Why:

- needs more careful product, policy, and trust design

## 10. UX Implications

Promorang should not describe all moments the same way.

### Food and gathering-heavy UX

Use language like:

- join tonight
- gathering live
- founder table
- energy rising

### Retail and drop UX

Use language like:

- unlock this drop
- visit store
- try-on reward
- founder access

### Service UX

Use language like:

- keep your ritual
- verify visit
- preferred slot
- loyal client status

### Grocery and CPG UX

Use language like:

- basket unlock
- recipe path
- try and save
- family ritual

Promorang should keep a unified backend model while letting category-specific UX copy differ.

## 11. Agent and API Implications

Agents should be able to query:

- which categories are near the user
- which moment archetypes best fit the user’s intent
- which categories require purchase vs simple visit vs service completion
- which opportunities are best for loyalty, status, savings, or creator discovery

This means category and archetype should be first-class in the agent surface.

Example agent queries:

- "Find a low-friction ritual moment near me today."
- "Show fashion drops with founder status potential."
- "Find grocery sampling moments with family rewards."
- "Find a beauty service moment with repeat-visit perks."

## 12. Bottom Line

Promorang should think of itself as a platform for:

`programmable real-world interactions`

not only:

- events
- meetups
- tasks

Events are one class of moment.

Retail, grocery, service, beauty, fitness, creator, and community behaviors are also valid moment categories when they are designed as:

- clear actions
- clear proof
- clear rewards
- clear memory outcomes

The platform becomes much larger and more defensible once moments are understood as:

`place-based behavior programs with proof, loyalty, and attribution`
