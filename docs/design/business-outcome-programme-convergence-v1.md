## Purpose

PROMORANG must work for businesses that already know the activation they want **and** for businesses that only know the business change they need.

The commercial experience therefore begins from one of three valid entry modes:

```
GOAL-LED     "Help me reach an outcome."
INTENT-LED   "I know what I want to run."
MARKET-LED   "Show me what people want."
```

All three converge on the same canonical response, participation and evidence systems.

## Core commercial chain

```
BUSINESS NEED
→ OUTCOME BRIEF
→ RECOMMENDED PROGRAMME
→ CONFIGURATION
→ CANONICAL RESPONSE OBJECTS
→ DISTRIBUTION
→ PARTICIPANT ACTION
→ EVIDENCE
→ DECISION
→ RETURN
```

A Programme is a human-facing recipe. It is **not** a new canonical market object and it is never proof that an outcome will happen.

## Zero → Hero definition

A commercial user reaches Hero when they have:
1. named a real business change,
2. configured a real response,
3. put it into market,
4. received attributable or verified evidence appropriate to the selected action,
5. made a clear next decision.

Signup, dashboard entry and campaign creation alone are not Hero states.

These are buying-language lenses. They compile into existing PROMORANG capabilities.

- First 50
- Fill the Room
- Try This
- Move This
- Quiet Hours
- Bring Them Back
- What Do They Want?
- Tell Somebody

A recipe can recommend an audience, participation action, participant value, distribution path and evidence requirement. The operator must still approve real terms, inventory, funding, eligibility, claims and fulfillment.

## Commercial continuity

Preferred acquisition path:

```
MARKETING PAGE
→ CHOOSE OUTCOME
→ ANSWER MINIMUM BRIEF
→ SEE RECOMMENDED PROGRAMME
→ SAVE / AUTH
→ RETURN TO SAME BRIEF
→ CONFIGURE
```

Auth must not erase the commercial decision already made.

## Truth boundaries

- Programme ≠ guaranteed result.
- Recommendation ≠ operator approval.
- Target ≠ forecast.
- Want ≠ supply.
- Response ≠ outcome.
- Claim ≠ use, visit or purchase.
- RSVP ≠ attendance.
- Proof submission ≠ verification.
- Verified action ≠ financial ROI unless supported by the underlying record.
- Marketing copy may describe what a programme is designed to pursue, but must not promise that the target will be reached.

## Marketing hierarchy

The participant homepage remains participant-first.

Commercial surfaces should use this hierarchy:
1. **What do you need to make happen?**
2. Outcome choices
3. Programme recommendation
4. How PROMORANG works
5. Live market context
6. Role/business-type detail
7. Commercial scope / pricing

Sector and stakeholder role are context, not the first question.

## Product hierarchy

Authenticated Brand and Merchant experiences should separate:

**Goal layer**
- what the business is trying to change,
- recommended/current programmes,
- relevant Wants,
- next decision.

**Operations layer**
- campaigns,
- offers/products,
- validation,
- orders,
- places,
- analytics/evidence.

The operations layer remains available to sophisticated users.

## Compiler contract

The Business Outcome Brief should compile into the existing PromoPilot / DemandPlan flow.

Do not build a parallel campaign engine.

Structured brief fields may be transformed into a natural-language planning statement for the existing compiler until a typed compiler input is justified.

## Persistence

The v1 outcome brief may be held as a versioned browser draft for pre-auth continuity.

This draft is not canonical market truth. It becomes durable operational truth only when the user explicitly saves/creates the existing campaign/response records.

## Design Lab expression

Outcome selection should feel like choosing a destination, not configuring SaaS.

Programme recommendation should feel like receiving a considered route or playbook.

Use PROMORANG tangible-object and Current/Return language where useful, but do not turn business outcomes into invented live market states.

## v1 success criteria

A cold business visitor can:
1. enter from `/solutions`, `/for-brands`, `/for-merchants`, `/join`, or the homepage business bridge;
2. choose an outcome without knowing PROMORANG mechanics;
3. define success in a few questions;
4. receive a programme recommendation;
5. authenticate without losing that brief;
6. continue into the existing campaign compiler;
7. retain direct access to power-user and market-led paths.
