# Promorang Platform Experience Map

This map defines which product mechanics belong where. The goal is to make Promorang feel like a social opportunity marketplace instead of a dashboard suite.

## Core Rule

Do not choose a layout because it is fashionable. Choose the mechanic that matches the user behavior.

Every major surface should answer three questions:

1. What can I do here?
2. Why does it matter now?
3. What do I receive if I help move it?

## Repeating Product Loop

`Discover -> Act -> Prove -> Earn -> Rank -> Back -> Repeat`

This loop should remain visible across roles, but the emphasis changes:

- Participants discover, act, earn, rank, and redeem.
- Creators launch, recruit, observe, reward, and reuse momentum.
- Hosts and venues convert attention into attendance and local proof.
- Brands and merchants sponsor, target, verify, and fund incentives.
- Promoters distribute, prove reach, and earn reputation or cash value.

## Stakeholder Experience Rule

Every major object in Promorang should expose a stakeholder value lens. A content drop, Moment, mission, offer, PromoShare pool, or Piece should not be designed only for the person viewing it.

Each object should make clear:

- Creator: what they are launching, proving, earning, and learning.
- Participant: what they can do, earn, redeem, rank for, or remember.
- Host: how attention becomes attendance, check-in, community proof, and repeat demand.
- Venue: how foot traffic, dwell time, redemptions, and audience fit become visible.
- Brand: how sponsored value moves people toward verified action instead of empty impressions.
- Merchant: how offers, purchases, redemptions, and loyalty loops are created.
- Promoter: how distribution work is attributed, ranked, and compensated.
- Platform/Admin: how trust, abuse prevention, liquidity, and system health are monitored.

This does not mean every screen shows every stakeholder equally. It means the interface should reveal who benefits and why the object is worth moving.

## Surface Mechanics

| Surface | Primary Mechanic | Secondary Mechanic | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Momentum | Social opportunity feed | Receipt rail, exchange rows | Shows what is moving and how the user can participate. |
| Content Drops | Feed plus job board | Creator post, referral ticket | Users need to browse drops, choose actions, and redistribute with attribution. |
| Drop Detail | Creator launch page | Task checklist, proof receipt | A drop should feel like a live content launch with clear ways to help. |
| Pulse | Local/social feed | Map, threshold progress | Moments are time, place, and social proof driven. |
| Moment Detail | Event page plus community room | Check-in, offer wallet, proof trail | Converts online intent into attendance and verified participation. |
| Missions | Jobs/task board | Application and completion states | Missions need clear asks, eligibility, deadline, proof, and payout. |
| PromoShare | Rewards wallet | Lottery/contest receipt, sponsor pool | Users need to understand how entries were earned and what they can win. |
| Pieces | Ecommerce plus exchange | Watchlist, order/claim confidence | Users need product clarity and market movement without a cold trading UI. |
| Vault | Personal wallet | Memories, perks, credentials | This is the user's retained value, not an analytics screen. |
| Discover | Social browsing | Search, filters, collections | Exploration should feel approachable and visual. |
| Creator Studio | Launch workspace | Analytics receipts | Creators need operational tools, but anchored to launches and outcomes. |
| Brand/Merchant Workspace | Campaign marketplace | Analytics, sponsorship funding | Operators need control, but the first layer should be opportunities and results. |
| Admin | Dashboard | Tables, moderation queues | True dashboard language is acceptable for internal operations. |

## Device Strategy

### Mobile

Mobile should behave like a social action app.

- Default to vertical feeds, action cards, claim tickets, sticky bottom actions, and compact receipts.
- Keep one dominant call to action per screen.
- Use bottom navigation for frequent participant movement.
- Collapse analytics into summary receipts and expandable panels.
- Avoid dense tables, multi-column control panels, and tiny exchange numbers.

### Tablet

Tablet should behave like a split-view marketplace.

- Use two-column layouts where the left side is discovery and the right side is receipt/action context.
- Let cards become wider editorial rows.
- Keep touch targets generous.
- Use sticky action panels only when they do not crowd the content.

### Desktop

Desktop can expose depth without becoming a dashboard.

- Use feed plus right rail for Momentum, Content Drops, Pulse, PromoShare, and Discover.
- Use comparison rows, market rows, and analytics drawers for deeper evaluation.
- Keep operational reporting behind secondary tabs or detail views.
- Prefer visible trails, receipts, and opportunity states over generic metric grids.

## Mechanic Rules

### Feed

Use feeds when the user needs freshness, discovery, social proof, or cultural motion.

Good for:

- Content Drops
- Pulse
- Discover
- Momentum
- Community activity

Avoid feed-first structure when the user is configuring, reconciling, moderating, or completing a linear workflow.

### Jobs Board

Use jobs/task mechanics when the product is asking the user to complete a specific contribution.

Required states:

- Open
- Claimed
- In progress
- Proof submitted
- Verified
- Paid/rewarded
- Disputed or expired

### Coupon/Offer

Use coupon mechanics when value can be claimed, redeemed, unlocked, sponsored, or expires.

Required states:

- Locked
- Eligible
- Claimed
- Redeemed
- Expired
- Sponsored

### Marketplace

Use ecommerce mechanics when users compare value, inspect details, back something, save, collect, or purchase.

Required states:

- Available
- Trending
- Backed
- Sold out or closed
- Watchlisted
- Recommended based on activity

### Exchange

Use exchange mechanics when value changes over time and needs confidence, movement, rank, or liquidity.

Required states:

- Price or weight movement
- Volume
- Holder/backer count
- Risk or eligibility note
- User position
- Watchlist or alert

### Analytics

Use analytics as proof, not as the first impression.

Analytics belongs in:

- Creator Studio
- Brand/Merchant Workspace
- Admin
- Expandable receipts
- Detail pages after the user understands the opportunity

## Promorang Signature Components

These should become reusable interface patterns:

- Opportunity Feed Item: content, moment, mission, or offer with a clear action and reward.
- Momentum Trail: signal, action, proof, reward, upside.
- Contribution Receipt: what the user did and what changed because of it.
- PromoShare Ticket: entry count, source, eligibility, sponsor pool.
- Local Landing Card: where online attention can become real-world proof.
- Piece Market Row: product clarity plus exchange confidence.
- Stakeholder Rail: creator, host, brand, venue, and user incentives on the same object.
- Stakeholder Value Map: a compact view of who benefits from an object and what proof Promorang captures for them.

## Decision Test

Before designing any surface, answer:

1. Is this for discovery, action, redemption, backing, or analysis?
2. What is the primary object: content, moment, mission, offer, ticket, piece, or receipt?
3. What is the user's next best action?
4. What proof does Promorang capture?
5. What incentive is created for the user and the stakeholder?
6. How does the layout simplify on mobile?
7. Which stakeholders are primary, secondary, and hidden-but-accounted-for on this surface?
