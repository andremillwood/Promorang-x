# PromoCard Product Positioning v1

Status: active design/product contract for the participation-market branch.

## Core hierarchy

**PromoCard is the participant product. PROMORANG is the network that makes it useful.**

Participants should not need to understand the full marketplace architecture before receiving value.

They should understand:
- what they want;
- what is open for them;
- what they picked up or committed to;
- what they actually did, used, earned or kept;
- what changed since the last time they looked.

Operators may use deeper PROMORANG tools, but those tools exist to create, distribute, fulfill, verify and learn from things that become meaningful to participants.

## Participant promise

**Your wants. Your access. Your moves. One PromoCard.**

Longer:
PromoCard is your personal place in PROMORANG: tell us what you want, keep what matters close, see when something opens, and carry the useful parts of your participation forward.

## Network promise

**PROMORANG turns what people want into things they can do.**

PROMORANG connects:
- participants expressing Wants;
- Discoveries that create or clarify preference;
- brands, merchants, creators, hosts and communities that can respond;
- real Offers, Moments, access and activations;
- claims / reservations / commitments;
- proof-backed follow-through;
- return and history.

## PromoCard states

The participant-facing card should prefer:

1. **Wanted** — things the participant expressed interest in or backed.
2. **Watching** — things the participant explicitly wants PROMORANG to bring them back to.
3. **Open** — real Offers, access, Moments or other responses currently available.
4. **Active** — things picked up, reserved, claimed or committed to that still have a next move.
5. **Kept** — used access, completed participation, earned value, memories or history worth retaining.

These are presentation groups, not permission to collapse canonical backend objects.

## Truth boundaries

- Wanted does not mean available.
- Watching does not mean entitled.
- Target met does not mean Open.
- Open does not mean claimed.
- Active does not mean completed.
- Claimed does not mean used.
- RSVP does not mean attendance.
- Proof submitted does not mean verified.
- Purchase must only be shown when purchase evidence exists.
- Kept must describe an actual retained state or confirmed history.

## Homepage contract

The first viewport should establish PromoCard, not explain the whole ecosystem.

Recommended order:
1. PromoCard hero.
2. Participant product showcase.
3. Live Wants / what people care about.
4. Ask / tell PROMORANG what is missing.
5. Discovery / cultural world.
6. What can Open.
7. Return / history.
8. Operator invitation.

Hero jobs:
- name PromoCard;
- explain PROMORANG as the network underneath;
- show the card visually;
- give one participant CTA: **Get my PromoCard**;
- give one market CTA: **See what people want**.

## Stakeholder marketing contract

Brand:
**See what people want. Give them something worth doing.**

Merchant:
**See what people nearby want. Open something they can use.**

Creator:
**Move attention toward something real enough to keep.**

Host:
**Turn interest into a Moment people want on their PromoCard.**

Community:
**Give shared interests continuity people can carry.**

Agency / enterprise:
**Coordinate what opens across clients, teams or locations while participant truth remains consistent.**

## Commercial relationship to PromoCard

Do not sell PromoCard as an advertising inbox.

A brand or merchant does not get to push arbitrary inventory onto a participant card.

A response can appear on PromoCard when there is a legitimate relationship such as:
- the participant expressed or watched a related Want;
- the participant explicitly saved / followed / joined a relevant object;
- eligibility or access was actually issued;
- the participant claimed or reserved something;
- the participant completed an action that creates a legitimate return path.

Consent, relevance and canonical eligibility remain required.

## Design Lab expression

PromoCard should feel like a tangible cultural object, not a SaaS dashboard.

Use:
- membership/pass/card physicality;
- editorial hierarchy;
- visible state changes;
- Current / movement only where real;
- receipts for consequence and history;
- scarcity only from authoritative inventory;
- culture imagery around the card, not generic fintech graphics.

Avoid:
- metric grids as the primary participant experience;
- crypto/wallet framing;
- fake badges;
- fabricated trending language;
- feature-list explanations;
- operator jargon in participant copy.

## Success test

A first-time visitor should be able to answer, within one viewport:

1. What do I get? **A PromoCard.**
2. Why would I want it? **It helps me keep what I want, see what opens and carry my participation forward.**
3. What is PROMORANG? **The network that makes the card useful.**
4. What should I do now? **Get my PromoCard or add my voice to something I want.**
