# PROMORANG Brand Grammar v1

Status: selected visual direction for Design Lab exploration on `design/promorang-design-lab`.

## Brand thesis

**Movement that comes back with value.**

PROMORANG is not defined by nightlife, coupons, points, or a single market. The brand should make a broad product system feel coherent by repeating a small number of meanings:

1. **Move** — an opportunity enters a person's path.
2. **Explore** — people discover, cross, try, share, visit, book, buy, attend, or participate.
3. **Return** — value comes back to the person or market.
4. **Proof** — PROMORANG can verify what actually happened.
5. **Kept** — useful value, access, provenance, memory, or identity remains.

The P is the person / PROMORANG anchor. The return arc is movement that leaves and comes back. Tracks are journeys and relationships. Marks are proof of movement.

## Core visual grammar

### The P

The PROMORANG P is not only a logo lockup. It is the anchor of the object system and the visual origin of movement.

Use it for:
- product identity
- PromoCard and PromoKey object marks
- issuer/trust relationships where PROMORANG is the credential layer
- start/end points in motion studies

Do not:
- repeat the P on every row or card
- use it as a substitute for clear merchant identity
- let the PROMORANG mark visually overpower the opportunity itself

### Return Arc

The Return Arc expresses the central economic and emotional promise: movement returns with something useful.

Use it for:
- state transitions such as save → kept, use → proof, move → return
- subtle credential/object details
- selected motion or loading moments
- campaign/world treatment where a journey needs one recognizable brand gesture

Do not:
- paste a boomerang shape behind every heading
- turn navigation into decorative motion
- use the arc where it does not communicate movement or return

### Tracks

Tracks are path-like signals that communicate movement, proximity, connection, or provenance.

Use them for:
- movement through places
- referral / crew / social context
- journey/progress
- map or place context
- provenance between an opportunity and the proof it creates

Tracks should remain sparse. They are semantic, not wallpaper.

### Marks

PROMORANG uses a stable mark vocabulary:

- **Move** — opportunity enters your path.
- **Explore** — discover, cross, try and act.
- **Return** — value comes back.
- **Proof** — verified record of what happened.
- **Kept** — retained value or meaning.

Temporary implementation may use the closest available icon-library symbols. Final brand asset work can replace those symbols while preserving their meanings.

## Cultural guardrail

The visual language may draw from broad principles such as earth, terrain, paths, rhythm, circular movement, return, repeated marks, dot cadence, and storytelling through movement.

It must **not** copy identifiable Aboriginal, Torres Strait Islander, or other Indigenous artworks, sacred symbols, clan marks, traditional patterns, or culturally specific iconography. PROMORANG should develop an original mark-making system grounded in its own product meanings and Caribbean/global context.

Do not describe production graphics as “Aboriginal patterns.” Use language such as:
- earth/path influence
- return marks
- track rhythm
- terrain-inspired texture
- movement/provenance system

If PROMORANG later commissions work directly inspired by a specific Indigenous tradition, it should be created in collaboration with and appropriately credited/compensated to artists or communities from that tradition.

## Palette

Night, focus, stage.

### Clay `#7A2E17`
Warmth, people, grounded context.

### Ochre `#FF6A00`
Movement and action.

### Sand `#EADCC6`
Paper, proof, retained objects.

### Gold `#F6C453`
Access, value, earned significance.

### Water `#4CC6F0`
Information, orientation, verified issuer cue.

### Leaf `#22C55E`
Live, valid, verified state.

The palette is elemental but should still behave like a contemporary consumer product. Utility remains quiet and readable.

## Typography

- **Fraunces** owns one major editorial moment at a time.
- **DM Sans** handles utility, trust, metadata, navigation and most secondary opportunity titles.
- Do not turn every content title into a large serif statement.
- Minimum body/utility sizing should remain legible on actual mobile screens; do not use low-contrast microtype for essential information.

## Photography

PROMORANG should feel like a world of real people, real places and real opportunity.

Photography should:
- lead Today / Discover when visual context materially helps
- favor people in context rather than generic isolated stock poses
- use strong editorial crops
- support merchants and issuers rather than hide them
- maintain natural skin and product rendering
- eventually be replaced by merchant/creator/user-owned media where available

PROMORANG overlays should be restrained. The brand should not make every photo orange.

## Object family

### PromoCard
Active access credential. It should show issuer, benefit, validity, state and holder clearly.

### Ticket / Pass
Access to a Moment or reserved experience. Paper/tactile language is appropriate.

### Receipt / Proof
Verified consequence. Should read as evidence, not analytics.

### Relic / Piece
Something culturally or personally worth keeping. Provenance matters.

### PromoKey
Compact access object for gated/digital opportunities. It can be visually denser and smaller than PromoCard.

The object family should look related without sharing one generic card component.

## Motion principles

Motion follows product meaning:

- **Save → Kept**: an object leaves the world surface and returns into the user's retained collection.
- **Use → Proof**: the credential moves forward toward validation; a receipt/proof object returns afterward.
- **Explore → Move**: contextual opportunity grows in salience when approached or selected.
- **Return**: circular/curved motion can complete when a person revisits a merchant, place or Scene.

Motion should remain brief and purposeful. Never animate purely to demonstrate the brand.

## Consumer application

### Today
World-first. One opportunity should dominate. Return language can explain why it surfaced and what remains after action.

### Discover
Use mixed content formats. Tracks / marks can reveal relationships, nearby movement and social context, but the actual opportunities remain the visual focus.

### Card
Object-first. PromoCard should carry the brand grammar more strongly than general utility screens.

### Vault
This is where marks, receipts, tickets, relics and provenance can become richest because the user is intentionally looking at retained objects.

### You
Identity emerges from verified movement and what has been kept. Avoid badge walls and generic gamification.

## Merchant / creator / operator application

The brand grammar should become quieter in workspaces:
- use the P and mark system for state and provenance
- keep data/operations legible
- avoid turning admin surfaces into themed art
- merchant identity must remain visible and respected

## Honesty rule

Design Lab examples may use illustrative merchants, ratings, availability, distance, social activity and verification cues to test hierarchy. Production must never imply a rating, verification, scarcity, proximity, friend activity, merchant relationship or availability state that is not supported by real data.

## Production gate

Before a consumer surface inherits Brand Grammar v1, confirm:

- the opportunity remains more important than decoration
- the merchant/issuer remains visible
- the P/arc/mark is used semantically
- one editorial hero owns the viewport
- utility remains readable
- state language is precise
- real data backs trust signals
- the interface still works without nightlife content
- object states remain understandable without relying on color alone
- the visual result could be recognized as PROMORANG even with the wordmark removed
